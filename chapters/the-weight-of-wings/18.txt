She woke up like someone who had been abducted by aliens, interrogated extensively, and then dropped back into bed before sunrise.

She blinked at the ceiling.

Where am I?

For a brief moment, the question felt entirely reasonable.

The blanket was still wrapped around her legs, warm and comfortable. She stretched both arms above her head and let out a satisfied sigh.

Sleep had done its job, after a rough day.

Morning light drifted through the room in intervals as the curtains swayed gently with the wind, brightening the room and then softening again.

Her arms dropped back onto the bed.

One immediately reached for her phone.

Got it.

She unlocked it while scratching her head and suppressing another yawn.

7:00.

Sia stared at the screen.

Her bulb was still flickering.

She blinked once.

Then twice.

Before she understood what it meant.

"It's seven?!"

The blanket was launched across the room.

Sia shot out of bed.

The next fifteen minutes happened with remarkable inefficiency.

Bathroom.

Wardrobe.

Bathroom again.

Searching for something she'd just put down.

Finding it in her hand.

Getting dressed.

Ignoring several complaints from her hair.

At 7:25 she locked the front door and ran — forgetting entirely about the door handle that benefited from checking.

She got the train. She reached the company premises only slightly out of breath.

— — —

As the building came into view she slowed her pace.

Straightened her posture.

Adjusted her bag.

Smoothed her clothes.

Professional.

Very professional.

She had just stepped through the entrance when —

"Sia."

She turned around. Not expecting it.

The smile arrived before she'd made any decision about it — the kind that appeared when the eyes had seen something familiar. Something they had been, quietly, looking forward to.

Veer was standing a few steps away.

"Guess both of us are late today."

Sia looked at him properly.

His shirt wasn't tucked in correctly.

His tie looked like it had lost a fight.

And there was a faint look of concentration on his face as he attempted to fix both problems at once.

It was not the composed image she usually saw.

Which somehow made it better.

"Good morning," she said, unable to stop smiling.

"Haha, yeah. I think we are."

— — —

The elevator arrived.

Both stepped inside.

The doors slid shut.

Sia became suddenly aware that she was breathing a little harder than normal.

She adjusted her grip on her bag.

Straightened her back.

Stood a little taller.

A quick glance to the side.

Veer was still trying to fix his tie while tucking in the stubborn corner of his shirt.

She looked away immediately.

A small smile lingered on her face.

Like she had accidentally witnessed something rare.

Something oddly precious.

And looking for too long would have felt unfair.

The elevator chimed.

The doors opened into the office.
