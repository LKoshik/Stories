Dhruv pulled on his jacket at the door. Riya stepped out in her pink hoodie, and the cold air hit her cheeks at once.

"Cold?"

"No."

Her nose was already pink.

Ten steps later, both her arms were wrapped around his left one, her head resting on his shoulder.

The air was crisp, carrying the faint smells of damp earth, distant food stalls, and someone's wood fire burning somewhere beyond the houses. Streetlights stretched along the road in soft pools of yellow, leaving the spaces between them dim enough for the sky to show through.

Above them, the moon hung quietly between scattered clouds, its pale light mixing with the warmer glow from the streets. Stars had begun to appear one by one, tiny points of light scattered across the dark sky.

Riya tilted her head back to look.

Dhruv looked too.

They walked like that for a while.

He breathed in. The cold air reached deep into his lungs and stung a little. Beside him, Riya took a deep breath, coughed once, and held on to his arm tighter.

He drew her closer to his side and quietly shifted his arm so that she could hold on more comfortably.

They continued walking for a while without speaking, their footsteps settling into an easy rhythm against the pavement. Every now and then, Riya looked upward, following the stars between the branches and rooftops.

Then—

Click.

She turned.

"What did you just do?"

Dhruv was already looking at the phone.

"Nothing."

"Dhruv!"

He showed her.

Her hair was a mess, with one strand lying across her cheek. She hadn't been ready at all.

"Delete that!"

"No."

"Take another!"

"Nope." He was already laughing, already putting the phone away.

She glared at him.

"Huuhh! Fine. I'll take it myself."

She unwound one arm from his—with visible reluctance, the cold rushing into the gap—and pulled out her phone.

Front camera.

She tilted her chin, tucked a strand behind her ear, undid it, tried the left side, then the right. She checked the moon over her shoulder, pouted slightly, then un-pouted, before deciding against both.

Dhruv watched.

The phone had been out for a full minute now, and not a single photograph had been taken.

He looked at this little creature, entirely absorbed in her own reflection, moonlight caught in her hair, and a grin tugged at the corner of his lips.

He flicked her forehead.

"Ow!"

Her hand flew to the spot and started rubbing it.

"What was that for?"

Dhruv raised an eyebrow and glanced at the phone in her hand.

Riya looked at the phone. Looked at him. Looked at the phone again.

Her eyes went wide.

"I didn't forget."

She grabbed the front of his jacket and yanked him down. "Come here."

He went, laughing, folding himself lower and lower until his cheek was pressed against hers.

Click.

Click.

Click.

The third, she decided, was acceptable.

She let go of his jacket, satisfied.

Dhruv straightened slowly, rubbing his neck.

"Happy?"

"Hm." She was already tucking her hand back around his arm. "Very."

They continued walking.

Every now and then, a couple passed them going in the opposite direction. Some walked hand in hand, while others talked quietly.

Then Riya noticed something.

She was surrounded by taller girls.

Much taller girls.

Her eyes followed one couple as they passed. The girl was nearly the same height as her boyfriend.

Riya's cheeks slowly puffed.

"Hmph."

She turned her head away.

Dhruv glanced at her with a puzzled look, then shook his head and looked ahead.

— — —

A sudden hiss from somewhere near the wall caught their attention.

Two cats were facing each other in the middle of the pavement, their backs arched and fur standing on end as they carried on with what appeared to be a very serious argument.

Riya slowed down, watching them with interest.

Then, without warning, one of them broke away and sprinted straight toward them.

Riya jumped aside.

"Hey! Where are you coming?"

The cat shot past her and disappeared behind Dhruv's legs.

Dhruv looked down.

Riya stared at the tiny face peeking out from behind him.

"Well..."

Dhruv crouched slowly, turning toward the cat and extending his hand.

"Hey. It's okay."

The cat vanished.

Just like that.

A blur of fur disappeared into the darkness.

Silence.

Dhruv remained crouched there, his hand still hanging in the air.

Riya lasted two seconds.

Then she burst into laughter.

She stepped closer, bent slightly, and began patting the top of Dhruv's head.

"Poor baby. It's okay. There, there."

He simply accepted his fate.

Finally, Dhruv stood up with a long sigh.

"Come on."

She slipped her arm back around his, and they started walking home. The street had grown quieter now; traffic hummed somewhere in the distance, warm light spilled from apartment windows, and the occasional sound of a dog or passing vehicle drifted through the cold air.

Riya leaned her head against his shoulder again, and neither of them said anything as they walked beneath the moon and scattered stars, slowly making their way back home.
