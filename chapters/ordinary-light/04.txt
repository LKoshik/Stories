The city was alive.

People poured out of office buildings and shops, all moving with the same destination in mind.

Home.

Bright LED screens flashed advertisements above crowded streets. Children pointed excitedly at things they wanted while exhausted parents negotiated impossible deals. Street vendors called out to passing customers. Restaurants glowed warmly beneath neon signs. Horns, conversations, footsteps, engines —

The entire city seemed lit from within.

And somewhere in the middle of all that evening chaos, a small car made its way through the traffic.

The windows were rolled up. The air conditioner hummed softly. A warm cabin light glowed overhead. The noise of the city reached them only as a distant murmur, softened by glass and distance until it became something almost peaceful.

In the passenger seat sat a person whose attention refused to stay in one place, licking an ice cream as her eyes wandered from one passing scene to another.

She watched people crossing roads. A man balancing three bags in one hand while talking on the phone. A little girl spinning in circles beside her father while waiting for the signal to change.

Then something else caught her attention.

Her eyes lingered.

A young mother stood near a bus stop. Curled against her shoulder was a tiny child, fast asleep. One small hand was wrapped tightly around the fabric of her mother's dress.

The child slept peacefully, completely unaware of the restless city around her. Cars moved. People hurried. Lights flashed. The world continued.

And she slept through all of it. With the absolute certainty that she was safe with her mother.

A small smile appeared on Riya's face.

She watched them until they slipped past the window and disappeared behind them.

Riya let out a quiet sigh.

Almost absentmindedly, her hand came to rest on her stomach.

Then she looked at Dhruv.

A moment passed.

She turned back to her ice cream and took another lick.

The car took a turn.

"So," she said, "why were you late?"

Dhruv kept his eyes on the road. A small curve appeared at the corner of his mouth, as though he had been expecting the question.

"A colleague stopped me for marriage advice."

Riya blinked.

"Marriage advice?"

"Mm."

"Why?"

"He has no interest in getting married. His parents disagree."

Riya chuckled.

"Poor fellow." She shifted slightly in her seat. "What did you tell him?"

"I told him it was normal. And that he should make it clear he's only marrying someone he actually likes."

"Hm." She nodded thoughtfully. "But he wouldn't stop."

Dhruv sighed.

"We walked out of the building. I got into the car. Started the car."

A pause.

"He was still talking through the window."

Riya burst into laughter. The image was far too easy to imagine.

"Seems like you made a good friend."

"Maybe." Dhruv shook his head. "If only he showed the same level of interest in other people's work."

Riya nodded and returned to her ice cream.

— — —

A few minutes later the ice cream was gone.

Riya stretched lazily in her seat. Then she rested her head on the glove box.

Dhruv didn't even look. He was busy navigating evening traffic.

Children appeared from nowhere. Motorcycles materialized in impossible places.

— — —

Riya slowly tilted her head.

She stared at Dhruv — a few lines on his forehead, focused eyes catching everything on the road, breath slow in and out, a trimmed beard and moustache, slightly open lips.

She was studying him with great attention. So focused that Dhruv should probably enroll her in an art school.

Dhruv felt the weight of her stare and glanced at her briefly before returning his attention to the road.

He chuckled.

"What?"

Riya laughed and tilted her head to the other side.

"Nothing."

She cleared her throat like she was getting serious now, sat upright, and took her phone out.

Dhruv gave a quick glance. A thumbnail of a woman holding a pan, a cooked dish beside her. A recipe, he thought, and moved on.

— — —

After a long drive the car stopped in front of their home.

Riya opened the door and disappeared toward the house.

Dhruv watched her jog up the steps.

Then the front door opened.

She vanished inside.

Silence settled over the car.

Dhruv leaned back against the seat.

Where does she get all that energy from, he thought.

Not for the first time.
