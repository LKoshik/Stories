Dhruv dropped Riya at her office and headed to his own.

The day passed.

Meetings happened. Emails were answered. Deadlines survived.

And eventually, the clock crawled toward evening.

— — —

By 5:40, he was parked outside her office building, giving a short honk.

A little distance away stood a tiny person with folded arms.

One foot tapped the ground relentlessly. Her cheeks were puffed. And she was staring at his car with absolutely no intention of getting inside.

Dhruv couldn't help smiling.

It reminded him of being a child after school. The bell rings. You're excited. You wait. Five minutes pass. Then ten. And somewhere around the eleventh minute, a horrible thought appears.

Did Dad forget me?

The expression was exactly the same.

He let out a quiet chuckle.

Now where did I put it...

He opened his bag and searched. After a few seconds his fingers found what they were looking for.

Got it.

He pulled it out and held it against the window.

Riya narrowed her eyes. Then leaned forward slightly.

A moment later her eyes widened.

"Hmph."

She immediately turned her face away. Then, with great reluctance and absolutely no change in attitude whatsoever, began walking toward the car.

The ice cream had nothing to do with it. Obviously.

She opened the passenger door. Grabbed the ice cream from his hand.

"Hmph."

And sat down.

Dhruv watched quietly. The angry act was already beginning to crack.

— — —

Riya unwrapped the ice cream and was about to take a bite when she noticed him looking at her.

One eyebrow raised. Waiting.

She looked away immediately. Then slowly extended the ice cream toward him — like a police officer pointing a flashlight at a criminal.

"Here."

Dhruv accepted his reward and took a bite.

Instant regret.

"Aah—" He pulled back. "Uuh—" The cold shot straight through his teeth.

Beside him, Riya burst into laughter.

"Serves you right." Another laugh escaped her. "For being late."

Dhruv was too busy relocating the ice block inside his mouth to defend himself.

The car pulled away from the curb.

Outside, the city moved through its usual evening. Inside, Riya sat with complete concentration, carefully working through her ice cream as though a single drop falling would constitute a personal failure.

The anger had mysteriously disappeared.
