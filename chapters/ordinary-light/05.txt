The apartment door clicked shut behind Riya. She kicked off her boots without looking, sent her bag skidding onto the sofa, and vanished toward the bathroom before Dhruv had even locked the door behind him.

He toed off his own shoes more carefully, nudging hers into a neat line against the wall with his foot. From behind the closed bathroom door came the hiss of water. He looked around the living room — and pulled the vacuum cleaner out of the closet.

By the time Riya stepped out, wrapped in a towel and trailing steam, he was still running it over the rug near the window.

She changed into a pale pink hoodie and loose pyjama pants, and by the time she came back out, Dhruv had switched off the vacuum and gone in for his own shower.

Riya propped her phone against the spice rack, pulled up a recipe video, and moved around the kitchen gathering what she needed — onions, ginger, a fistful of coriander, the good pressure cooker. She'd assembled everything by the time she heard the bathroom door open again.

Dhruv came out in a deep blue T-shirt scattered with small stars, paired with cream-colored shorts, hair still damp, and walked straight into the kitchen.

Two hands crept in from behind Riya's shoulder, wiggling.

"At your service, mistress," said a voice doing a poor job of disguising itself as someone else's.

Riya snorted, swatting at the air without turning around.

"Then make yourself useful. Take the atta, knead it till it's soft. I'll do the vegetables."

Her knife working steadily through capsicum and carrot while his hands pressed and folded dough on the counter beside her. Outside the window, the evening had gone gold, the warm light making the small kitchen feel larger than it is. Somewhere between the chopping and the kneading, Riya started humming.

She swept the vegetables into the cooker and added water. Dhruv, dough finished, began rolling it into neat round balls, lining them up like a small dough army on the plate.

"Pass the salt," Riya said, sprinkling in the first of the spices without looking up.

There was a pause — just long enough that she glanced over — while Dhruv scanned the shelf, picked a bottle, and handed it to her.

She held it up.

Looked at him.

Then she burst out laughing.

"Dhruv. This is baking powder."

"I—"

He grabbed it back before she could finish, shoved it into place, and returned with the correct one.

"I'm just tired, okay."

"Ya, ya," she said, still grinning, and went back to the cooker.

"Anything else you need?" he asked, dusting flour off his palms.

"No, I've got the rest. Just put on something nice."

He washed his hands, dropped onto the sofa, and picked up the remote, squinting faintly as he scrolled — before he landed on Indila's "Love Story" and let it play.

From the kitchen, Riya began swaying to it, her head tipping side to side as she packed rolled chapatis into the hot box one after another, brushing the top of each with a thin coat of ghee.

Whatever she was doing with her shoulders had stopped being cooking and started being dancing, and Dhruv, half-watching over the back of the sofa, caught himself thinking he should probably take her to a dance class sometime — and smiled at the thought.

She carried the chapatis and the curry to the table, set out two plates, and they sat down together — a short, quiet prayer, then dinner.

It didn't last long. Riya slid the remote out from under Dhruv's unresisting hand somewhere between bites, and by the time she'd found a show worth watching, his plate was empty and she was still picking at hers while thumbing through channels.

Dhruv let out a small breath, shook his head, and got up to rinse his plate. Riya glanced at him, set the remote down, and hurried through what was left on hers.

He was at the sink, unmoved by the small creature of guilt now bumping gently against his side. No change in his face. He set the plate in the drying rack, walked past her, and sat down on the sofa.

She washed her own plate in record time, walked over, and dropped herself against him, arms wrapping tight around his waist, face pressed into his chest.

"What happened?" she asked, quiet now.

His arm came around her without hesitation. He looked down at her.

"Why don't you just hand me the remote when you don't know what to watch."

"I'm sorry, Dhruv. I was just messing around."

She swung her legs up onto the sofa, tightening her hold on him like that would make the apology more convincing.

He was quiet for a second.

Then, low:

"Let's go for a walk."

She was up before he'd finished the sentence, nodding.

He clicked off the TV and stood.
